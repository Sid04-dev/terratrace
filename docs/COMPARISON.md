# 🆚 How TerraTrace compares with existing tools

Judges will ask: *"Doesn't this already exist?"* Here's our honest answer. Use it on the "Why us" slide.

| Tool | Made by | What it's great at | What's missing for a student |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NASA Worldview** | NASA | Browse 1,200+ global satellite imagery layers and download the data | Shows imagery, not *trends*. No statistics, no plain-language answer, expert UI |
| **NASA Giovanni** | NASA GES DISC | Powerful maps + time series for many datasets | Steep learning curve. You need to know which dataset and settings to pick |
| **Copernicus Climate Pulse** | ECMWF / EU | Near-real-time global temperature on a globe, hover for values, anomaly vs absolute toggle | Global and daily focus. No "how has *my* place changed and is it significant?", no citations |
| **ERA Explorer** | ECMWF / Copernicus | Climate of any city, compare two cities | Climatology, not trend testing. No export for coursework |
| **Climate Reanalyzer** | Univ. of Maine | Expert reanalysis maps and series | Built for researchers |
| **Show Your Stripes** | Ed Hawkins, Univ. of Reading | Iconic one-glance warming stripes | Temperature only, no statistics, no other variables |
| **NOAA Climate Explorer** | NOAA | County-level charts | US only |

## Where TerraTrace is different
1. **It answers one question, for anywhere:** *"Is this place really changing, and by how much?"*
2. **Statistics built in:** every number is tested (Mann–Kendall + Sen's slope), and faded areas on the globe show where change is *not* significant. Most viewers just draw lines.
3. **Made for coursework:** fact sheet PDF, CSV, chart, stripes image, APA/Harvard/BibTeX citation, a methods paragraph and research questions. Nothing else on this list does that.
4. **Multi-variable fingerprint:** temperature, rainfall and soil moisture for the same place at once, each cross-checked against a second NASA record.
5. **Context:** "rising faster than 87% of places on Earth", "2024 was the 2nd highest year", and a Hotspots list.

## What we borrowed (and credit)
- Hover-to-read values and an absolute-vs-anomaly view, from Climate Pulse
- Layer opacity, from Worldview
- Climate stripes, from Ed Hawkins' #ShowYourStripes (credited on every stripes image)
- City comparison, from ERA Explorer

## Honest limits vs these tools
- Coarser grid (~550 km) and shorter record (2001–2024) than Climate Reanalyzer/Worldview
- No near-real-time data, and no future projections
- We point students to these tools for deeper work. That's a feature: TerraTrace is the **front door**.
